Genesis Owusu - Redstar Wu & the Worldwide Scourge

Genre: Post-Punk Revival, Political Hip Hop, Neo-Soul
Release Date: May 15, 2026
My Rating: 4.5/5

Review by BuckdeLuck

Creative concept, innovative genre fusions, and tight, poetic, and thematic songwriting? Yep, that's my Genesis Owusu.

Genesis Owusu - a Ghanian-Australian artist who began making the rounds in the online 2020's music scene with Smiling With No Teeth, a brilliant and bold debut record with overflowing, inspiring genre fusions and a hyperunique concept of racist/depressing black dogs antagonizing Genesis Owusu.

In 2023, he dropped Struggler, a bit of a sophmore slump. The concept of a struggling roach vs god retained uniqueness but lacked depth, while the genre fusions (now featuring post punk) felt very separated and like completely different detours rather than elaborate and blended as they were on SWNT.

Genesis Owusu took his time with this record, touring, locking himself in chapels, returning to Ghana for the first time in 11 years. You know, the usual.

However, the trip to Ghana has laid the foundations for this record. Being in his homeland again, and seeing the conditions (the good and bad), alongside the current state of the world, laid the foundations his third release, REDSTAR WU & THE WORLDWIDE SCOURAGE.

First, we gotta start with concept. There is this disease infecting the world called The Worldwide Scourge, a concept meant to represent all the systemic evil of the world (patriarchy, capitalism, facism, white supremacy, neoliberalism) and how it causes individuals (especially the elite in power) to perform actions with non-empathy, exploitation, consumerism, and desire for money. However, Redstar Wu is here to fight against this disease, and in doing so, hopefully inspire the people around and YOU to do the same.

Previous records placed Genesis in perspective of the oppressed and helpless, while now he may still face oppression but has the ability and drive to fight it. The Worldwide Scourge is a masterful concept in its own right, allowing Genesis to attack and deconstruct many aspects of life and the people who are pushing and embracing our increasingly alt-right society and also providing a more hopeful and inspiring overall listen.

But we need to talk music about an album right? Thankfully, Genesis brought the beautiful genre fusions back with a passion. Rap, pop, and neo-soul are still here, as expected, but with an expanded punk/post-punk repertoire that Struggler failed to employ effectively. Stampede is the peak example of this - Genesis rapping with a poppy hook, punk ethos, and synths that keep the punk going. The political ethos coincides well with punkier textures (Most Normal American Voter: is the perfect example of this, sounds like a synthier Dead Kennedys record).

The synths and guitars are plentiful and bursting with attitude, while the drums are pronounced and passionate Additionally, this is a Genesis Owusu album; subsequently, the hooks are government mandated to be earworms. He can bounce effortlessly between singing, and rapping, while doing the same with his genres, always presenting his musical and lyrical ideas with remarkable conviction.

REDSTAR WU & THE WORLDWIDE SCOURGE is everything I wanted from Genesis - singular and impactful concept and genre fusions, now with an aggressive, political edge that the modern world necessitates more than ever. This is a truly inspirational record, and an easy frontrunner for AOTY.

Protomartyr - Hotel Usona

Genre: Art Rock, Post-Punk
Release Date: September 25, 2026
My Rating: 4/5

Review by RichardWiggins

We got a good review.

I am a huge Protomartyr fan. I've lost years of my life to their music, have seen them live countless times and feel a great sense of occasion when they release a new album, not to mention a lot of nervous energy. 2023's Formal Growth in the Desert was not without its merits but it was my least favourite of their offerings to date. My immediate reaction was strangely middling. Last year I was entranced seeing them play one of their The Agent Intellect 10th anniversary shows, but it only compounded my fears that their best years were behind them. Listening to Hotel Usona this morning, those concerns couldn't be further in the rearview mirror. It is an album with qualities so obvious that it's an immediate contender for their best record. You only need to listen to the opening tracks to know it will be a huge success. "Welcome back!"

It gives me such a buzz to hear them trying (and nailing) a whole suite of new approaches across the record. The fiddly pedal-infused guitars on Converts provides one of the poppier moments with brighter melodies than they've meddled with in over a decade, instrumentally its unlike anything they've ever released. They have used brass instruments to bolster their sound before but they've never been as seamlessly integrated as on The Old Hotel. Then there's the surprisingly atmospheric, reverb drenched and at points even ambient Lavender - Gospel where the band are at the height of their powers dynamically. When they go from this to their more familiar wall of sound it reminds me of the sort of trick I'd expect from a post-rock group, but with totally different effect.

Dave Fridmann's flawless and consistently engaging production clearly deserves some credit. At no point does he fall into any particular groove, each song having something new and interesting to say, providing a nice counterpoint and tension to the surrounding tracks. I particularly love the menacing, overlapping whispering on The Knife Dream which is backed by the band and surrounding symphony to generous and overwhelming effect. I can hear some of the qualities Fridmann has brought to records by groups as varied as Mogwai, Magdalena Bay and MGMT, highlighting Protomartyr's lesser known aspects.

I'm used to Joe Casey's stark political and social commentary. What I'm not used to is him is telling a single story over the course of a whole album, more specifically the story of a run-down hotel and the experiences of its staff and guests. This device is a great success. On several occasions you can read through the lines and hear Casey telling a broader story about himself, the band and the state of America. It’s a brave and bold statement for a seventh album.

Hotel Usona a unique "must listen" in Protomartyr's discography for fans and non-fans alike. I'll be urging anyone who will listen to check it out.

Sarah Davachi - The Will of Tongues

Genre: Drone, Modern Classical
Release Date: August 28, 2026
My Rating: 4/5

Review by AudioRedux

Pure Beauty & Devastation

In my headcanon, this is the spiritual successor to "No Title as of 13 February 2024 28,340 Dead". It resoundingly captures pure devastation and aftermath-of-atrocity energy. Sarah Davachi crafts a world of disillusionment and desolation with these despondent, glacially paced tonal movements. Simply put, this album made me fall in love with minimal microtonal classical music again. Now, that isn’t to say that The Will of Tongues isn’t for the patient listener. This album rewards a careful and weary headspace (a mood I can get into almost instantly). Pieces here run over 23 minutes, interspersed with smaller “interludes,” but the real reprieves are the trilogy of holy chants throughout the album. “Songs of the Smile's Fig: I. Hours,” Songs of the Smile's Fig: II. Salts, " and " Songs of the Smile's Fig: III. Follies " Salts,” and “Songs of the Smile's Fig: III. Follies” all serve as epic payoffs for much of the smoke and fog the drone pieces take you through.

This record tosses you into a space to ponder the most beautiful but psychotic aspects of our world. All of this is expressed through long-form ascension and descension that navigate brass, organ, and flute with Sarah’s signature style and grandiosity. This world doesn’t sugarcoat our realities, and there isn’t a light at the end of this tunnel either—just another whimper that fades out into the never-ending universe. The best part about it? You don’t have to explore every minute of this two-hour odyssey to get something out of it (though I would advise at least one full listen to get a sense of the longing and scale). We may not like how the world is, but we can at least enjoy the art that’s inspired by it. One of the most powerful albums of the year if you give it an honest shot.

PISS - You Made Me / Skin on Linen

Genre: Noise Rock, Post-Hardcore
Release Date: October 30, 2026
My Rating: 4/5

Review by Elesh

9/10, Never Listening Again

Yeesh!

This album is joining Preacher's Daughter as the only 9 I don't want to listen to, but if I had to choose between the two to listen to right now I'm going with Ethel and it isn't close.

This feminism has more teeth than any feminism I've heard before. It is extremely uncompromising. There are no ands, ifs, or buts, no grey areas. There is only despair and rage. I don't know if I've heard an album with more disturbing, uncomfortable lyrics before, and delivered in a way that sounds so personal as well. The album is also very well read and full of poetry from influential feminist thinkers that it knows can carry context and meaning better than any song re-written about the same things, which is gutsy but absolutely pays off. The picture this album paints of the world and of the female experience is at once disgustingly vivid and fully formed, there are no gaps left for interpretation, there is only sharp, burning feeling and dread.

You cannot play this live! You just can't! Well you could, but are people going to be excited to go to a show where this music is being played? Pay money to attend? I can't imagine feeling that way, at least if you're a man. Are you going to bang your head to this? I wouldn't feel ok going that! You are certainly not going to dance or sing along. I think if this band came to my city I would pay the money for a ticket because they deserve it, then list it for free on stubhub or something so someone who feels like they can get something out of this experience can attend. To be clear none of this is meant to be negative, it's really meant to be a testament to how impressively hostile and totalizing this music is. If I were the singer I don't know if I could perform songs like Blocking a Scene.

Sort of looking into the context around this album and what people are saying about it, I was concerned, as it seemed to be feminist music in genres primarily (numbers wise) enjoyed and performed by men, not to mention getting a ton of traction on male dominated spaces like this site and the fantano channel. I was in that sense concerned that it would be the sort of album a man could enjoy and feel like a good feminist man for listening to. I am leaving this experience with that concern thoroughly dispelled. If you are a man and you can leave this album without feeling disgusted in any way I really don't know what to tell you. It is the furthest you could possibly get from something self-satisfying.

The world is extremely gross.
